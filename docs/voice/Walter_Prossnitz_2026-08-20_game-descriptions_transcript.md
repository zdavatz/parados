# Game descriptions — Walter's dictated blurbs (2026-08-20 voice messages)

Five voice messages, dictated in English, one per game. Walter identified them in
WhatsApp as: **DiS · DL · Makalaina · TIK · Where WAS that elbow?**
Transcribed from the `.m4a` files in this directory (whisper large-v3-turbo, verbatim).

These are the **front-page / marketing blurbs** (`index.html` game cards + the lead
sentence of each game's `README.md` entry) — they are *not* rules text. Each one now
also carries a player count and a playing time, which the cards show in the
`.players` line.

## 1 — Democracy in Space (`..._08-38_1.m4a`, 0:30)

> Democracy in Space, two players, duration 15 to 45 minutes.
> Based on a feature of the U.S. Electoral College and most parliamentary systems,
> this is area majority culled to its essence. A gentle opening suddenly transforms
> into a nail-biting finish. The unique tie-break condition needs to be kept in the
> back of your mind, but you might not know for a while if you'll need it this time.

## 2 — Divided Loyalties (`..._08-38_2.m4a`, 0:34)

> Divided Loyalties. Two players. Duration 40 to 80 minutes.
> Many turns are offensive and defensive and every single one has consequences.
> This is connect three or four but with six colors. Your color is always loyal to
> you, your opponent's never is. And the other four? Sometimes they are and
> sometimes they aren't. Tiles can even be loyal in one direction and disloyal in
> another.

Note: the old blurb's opening line ("Perhaps the craziest Connect Four game ever")
and closing line ("Not for the faint of heart") are **not** in the new dictation, so
they were dropped from the card. The craziest-Connect-Four line still opens the DL
**rules modal** (from the 2026-07-19 dictation) — that text was not changed.
Also new: "connect **3 or 4**" (was "connect 4"), which matches the actual bridges.

## 3 — MAKA LAINA (`..._08-40_3.m4a`, 0:33)

> Makalaina. Two players, duration 20 to 40 minutes. It's the first turn and the
> battle is on. No time to get warmed up in Makalaina. You need to be planning from
> the very start, evolving your long-term strategy, but staying flexible. The influx
> of new discs adds a good deal of randomness, which really spices things up. And
> later on, watch out for the end game trap, which can easily lead to a surprise
> winner.

Note: the old blurb ended "…even a small shift can have consequences"; the new one
replaces that with the randomness/endgame-trap idea.

## 4 — TIK — The Impatient Kangaroo (`..._08-41_4.m4a`, 0:26)

> The Impatient Kangaroo.
> The plan? To create a 21st century successor to the hit solo puzzle game Rush
> Hour. Many players think that hopping through the Outback collecting goodies is
> more fun than trying to shove your way through traffic. And, thanks to the
> program, there's all kinds of different ways to play.

Note: **no player count or duration** was dictated for this one (it is the only solo
game), so its card keeps "1 Player · Puzzle" with no time. "Rushhour" is now spelled
"Rush Hour", and "super-hit" is just "hit".

## 5 — Frankenstein / "Where WAS that elbow?" (`..._08-42_5.m4a`, 0:40)

> Where was that elbow? 1-4 players, duration 5-15 minutes.
> This silly little game has turned out to be quite a brain trainer. A number of
> players have reported a noticeable increase in their memory capacity after just a
> few plays. Build from the inside out or outside in. It's equally fun as a solo
> game or with up to three other friends. Our age recommendation is seven years and
> up. Don't be surprised if the youngest player wins.

Note: Walter opens with the title **"Where was that elbow?"** (and labelled the
WhatsApp message "Where **WAS** that elbow?"), so the game was **renamed** from
"Frankenstein — Where's that green elbow?" to **"Where WAS that elbow?"** (Zeno's
call, 2026-08-20: drop the Frankenstein name from the title). Localized per
variant — DE "Wo WAR dieser Ellbogen?", JP 「あのひじはどこだった？」, CN
《那只手肘刚才在哪里？》, UA «Де БУВ той лікоть?», IT «Dov'ERA quel gomito?».
The **filenames stay `frankenstein*.html`** (app update lists + shared links
depend on them), and the creature inside the game is still a Frankenstein
("Frankie", "Frankenstein body") — only the game's *title* changed.
The old "frankly memorable" pun line and the "shorter and sweeter than Rainbow"
line are gone (Rainbow Blackjack was delisted 2026-08-19 anyway).
